        How does it work?

        Every step below is computed by the model in this page as it classifies your text. "Show the steps" plays them in order; slow motion plays them slowly every time.

        1. The tokenizer splits the text

          The model never sees letters or words, only pieces from a fixed vocabulary of 30,522 pieces. Rare words are split into several pieces (marked with a blue edge); hover a piece for its number. [CLS] is added at the start: the classifier reads the model's output at that position.

        2. Layer by layer

          The pieces pass through 6 transformer layers. Each layer lets every piece look at the others and update itself. If we stop after a layer and apply the final classifier, we see what the model would answer at that depth: early layers usually do not know yet, and the answer forms on the way up.

        3. Where the classifier looks

          Attention: how strongly the [CLS] position draws on each piece, averaged over the layer's attention heads. Darker means more. Pick a layer. [CLS] and [SEP] often collect much of the attention as a resting place, so compare the word pieces with each other.

        4. From scores to probabilities

          The classifier gives one score per label (77 labels) and a softmax turns them into probabilities. The top five are below; the strip shows all of them, largest first. The small probabilities on other labels are what distillation passes on: the teacher's soft labels tell the student which labels are similar, not just which one is right.

          1. Which words matter

            Each word is removed in turn and the model runs again on what is left. Words whose removal lowers the probability of support it; words whose removal raises it count against it. Hover a word for the change.

          2. Teacher and student

            The student learned to imitate the soft labels of the teacher, Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (16 similar examples, 4-bit, probe). The teacher is far too large to run here, but its saved soft labels for the test texts are in this page.

          3. When to ask the teacher

            In a cascade the student answers and passes the texts it is least sure about to the teacher. The slider sets how many; accuracy and the confidence threshold are measured on the held-out test set.
